\chapter{Sixth-Order Resolution: Exact Capability and the Remaining Dynamical Question}

\section{Exact Invariant Count}

For the real irreducible $SO(3)$ representation $V_5$ of dimension eleven, the degree-$d$ invariant space is

\[
\operatorname{Inv}_d(V_5)=\left[\operatorname{Sym}^d(V_5)\right]^{SO(3)}.
\]

An independent exact Molien calculation reproduces the scalar multiplicities

\[
\dim\operatorname{Inv}_d(V_5)=1,0,1,0,2,0,6
\qquad(d=0,\ldots,6).
\]

The same combinatorial construction passes the raw-dimension sanity check

\[
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^d(V_5)=\binom{d+10}{10}
\]

at each degree before group averaging is applied. In particular,

\[
\dim\operatorname{Inv}_2=1,
\qquad
\dim\operatorname{Inv}_4=2,
\qquad
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{Inv}_6=6}.
\]

The vanishing values at degrees three and five are facts about this specific representation $V_5$. They must not be promoted into a general rule that odd-degree $SO(3)$ invariants always vanish; even-$\ell$ representations can admit scalar cubic invariants.

Let

\[
I_2(A)=\|A\|^2
\]

be the unique quadratic invariant. Multiplication by the nonzero polynomial $I_2$ is injective in the polynomial invariant ring, so the two-dimensional quartic invariant space generates a two-dimensional inherited sextic subspace:

\[
I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4\subset\operatorname{Inv}_6,
\qquad
\dim(I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4)=2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\dim\left(\operatorname{Inv}_6/I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4\right)=6-2=4.
}
\]

Degree six thus contains four genuinely new invariant directions beyond multiplication of the quartic algebra by the norm. On the unit sphere $I_2=1$, the inherited sector reduces to the same quartic information, so all genuinely new sixth-order shape resolution lies in those four primitive directions.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive sextic dimension]
For $V_5$, the degree-six invariant algebra has dimension six, while the inherited subspace $I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4$ has dimension two. Hence the primitive sextic quotient has dimension four.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is a dimension statement. By itself it proves that new sixth-order information exists; it does not yet prove that any particular new invariant resolves the two physical shape directions left flat at quartic order.

\section{The Quartic-Flat Shape Plane}

At the regular quartic minimum

\[
A_\star=\sqrt{\frac35}Y_{50}+\sqrt{\frac25}Y^c_{55},
\]

the five-dimensional tangent kernel decomposes into three infinitesimal rotations and two genuine shape directions. A convenient orthogonal basis of the nonrotational shape-flat plane is

\[
U=-\frac{\sqrt{14}}{14}Y^c_{52}+Y^c_{53},
\]

\[
V=\frac{\sqrt{14}}{14}Y^s_{52}+Y^s_{53},
\]

with

\[
\|U\|^2=\|V\|^2=\frac{15}{14},
\qquad
\langle U,V\rangle=0.
\]

The normalized directions are

\[
e_1=\sqrt{\frac{14}{15}}U,
\qquad
e_2=\sqrt{\frac{14}{15}}V.
\]

These vectors lie in the tangent space of the constrained quartic minimum manifold

\[
\mathcal M_4=S^{10}\cap\{A:B_2(A)=0\},
\qquad
B_2(A)=P_2(A\otimes A).
\]

\section{An Explicit Primitive Sextic Witness}

Define

\[
B_4(A)=P_4(A\otimes A),
\]

\[
C_8(A)=P_8(B_4(A)\otimes A),
\]

and the scalar degree-six invariant

\[
\boxed{
J_{4,8}(A)=\|C_8(A)\|^2
=\left\|P_8\left(P_4(A\otimes A)\otimes A\right)\right\|^2.
}
\]

Using the standard orthonormal complex spherical-harmonic convention and the corresponding real tesseral basis, an independent reconstruction from raw Clebsch--Gordan coefficients reproduces the same polynomial. At $A_\star$,

\[
J_{4,8}(A_\star)=\frac1{143},
\]

and the first derivatives along both shape-flat directions vanish:

\[
DJ_{4,8}(A_\star)[U]=DJ_{4,8}(A_\star)[V]=0.
\]

The remaining issue is not whether $J_{4,8}$ is stationary on the shape plane, but what its \emph{intrinsic} second derivative is on the curved manifold $\mathcal M_4$.

\section{Why the Ambient Hessian Is Not the Intrinsic Hessian}

A straight-line calculation through the ambient representation space answers a different question from the constrained calculation. For a tangent direction $U$, the ambient second derivative is

\[
D^2J_{4,8}(A_\star)[U,U]=\frac{547}{1001}.
\]

After normalizing $U$, this straight-line contribution becomes

\[
\frac{14}{15}\frac{547}{1001}=\frac{1094}{2145}.
\]

That number is correct as an ambient-Hessian restriction. It is not the intrinsic Hessian on $\mathcal M_4$, because a straight line immediately leaves the curved intersection of the sphere with $B_2=0$.

A constrained path must instead have the form

\[
A(\epsilon)=A_\star+\epsilon U+\frac12\epsilon^2W_U+O(\epsilon^3).
\]

The unit-sphere condition requires

\[
\langle A_\star,W_U\rangle=-\|U\|^2,
\]

while the quartic constraint requires

\[
DB_2(A_\star)[W_U]+2P_2(U\otimes U)=0.
\]

One exact solution is

\begin{align*}
W_U={}&-\frac{7\sqrt{15}}{30}Y_{50}
+\frac{\sqrt{21}}{21}Y^c_{51}
+\frac{\sqrt{21}}7Y^c_{54}
-\frac{13\sqrt{10}}{70}Y^c_{55},\\
W_V={}&-\frac{7\sqrt{15}}{30}Y_{50}
-\frac{\sqrt{21}}{21}Y^c_{51}
-\frac{\sqrt{21}}7Y^c_{54}
-\frac{13\sqrt{10}}{70}Y^c_{55}.
\end{align*}

For the mixed derivative, an exact second-order correction may be chosen as

\[
W_{UV}=\frac{\sqrt{21}}{21}Y^s_{51}-\frac{\sqrt{21}}7Y^s_{54},
\]

with

\[
\langle A_\star,W_{UV}\rangle=0,
\qquad
P_2(A_\star\otimes W_{UV})=-P_2(U\otimes V).
\]

For any such constrained path, the second derivative contains the second-fundamental-form correction:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2}{d\epsilon^2}J(A(\epsilon))\Big|_{0}
=D^2J(A_\star)[U,U]+DJ(A_\star)[W_U].
}
\]

The independently reproduced correction is

\[
DJ_{4,8}(A_\star)[W_U]=-\frac{43}{1001}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{547}{1001}-\frac{43}{1001}
=\frac{504}{1001}
=\boxed{\frac{72}{143}}.
\]

The same calculation gives

\[
d^2_{\mathcal M_4}J_{4,8}(V,V)=\frac{72}{143},
\qquad
d^2_{\mathcal M_4}J_{4,8}(U,V)=0.
\]

After normalizing the shape basis,

\[
\boxed{
H_{\mathrm{shape}}(J_{4,8})
=\frac{336}{715}I_2\succ0.
}
\]

The distinction between $1094/2145$ and $336/715$ is therefore not a discrepancy between two constructions of $J_{4,8}$. The first is the normalized ambient straight-line Hessian contribution. The second is the true constrained Hessian after the curvature of $\mathcal M_4$ is included.

\begin{theorem}[Sextic resolving-capability theorem]
For the $\ell=5$ representation, degree six introduces four primitive invariant directions beyond $I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4$. Moreover, the explicit invariant $J_{4,8}$ has positive-definite intrinsic Hessian on the two-dimensional nonrotational quartic-flat tangent space at $A_\star$:
\[
H_{\mathrm{shape}}(J_{4,8})=\frac{336}{715}I_2\succ0.
\]
Hence sixth-order invariant structure is not merely dimensionally capable of resolving the quartic degeneracy; an explicit sextic invariant resolves both physical shape-flat directions.
\end{theorem}

\section{What This Theorem Does Not Prove}

The theorem concerns the invariant algebra. It does not establish that the specific nonlinear PDE studied earlier generates a nonzero coefficient along this resolving primitive sextic direction. That is a separate center-manifold and dynamical-selection calculation.

A complete fifth-order amplitude reduction requires the higher slaved-mode corrections conventionally denoted $w^{(3)}$ and $w^{(4)}$, together with all contributions produced when those corrections are fed through the quadratic term $\nu\psi^2$ and cubic term $-g\psi^3$. It also requires the corresponding Clebsch--Gordan recouplings through intermediate channels. Those coefficients have not yet been derived in the canonical source.

The inferential boundary is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{algebraic resolving capability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{PDE dynamical selection}.
}
\]

\section{The Sharper PDE Target}

The next dynamical calculation need not begin by demanding a closed-form expression for every coefficient of the full sixth-order invariant energy. The immediate scientific question is whether the PDE lifts the two-dimensional quartic degeneracy. That question is controlled by the intrinsic sextic curvature restricted to the shape-flat plane:

\[
H_{6,\mathrm{shape}}^{\mathrm{PDE}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
 d^2H_6(e_1,e_1) & d^2H_6(e_1,e_2)\\
 d^2H_6(e_2,e_1) & d^2H_6(e_2,e_2)
\end{pmatrix}_{\mathcal M_4}.
\]

The first target is therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_{6,\mathrm{shape}}^{\mathrm{PDE}}\stackrel{?}{=}0.
}
\]

If the matrix vanishes, the quartic degeneracy survives to that order. If it is positive definite, sixth order locally lifts the degeneracy toward $A_\star$; if negative definite, it lifts it away; if indefinite, the two shape directions split differently. This restricted target does not eliminate the need for the higher-order slaving calculation, but it prevents the derivation from computing coefficients irrelevant to the first falsifiable question.

\section{A General Resolution Principle}

Let $\mathcal I_p$ be a family of invariants available at descriptive order $p$, and define

\[
x\sim_p y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(x)=f(y)\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal I_p.
\]

If

\[
\mathcal I_p\subseteq\mathcal I_q,
\]

then

\[
[x]_q\subseteq[x]_p.
\]

A richer invariant language can leave an equivalence class unchanged or refine it. If some $g\in\mathcal I_q$ distinguishes two states that every element of $\mathcal I_p$ identifies, then the inclusion is strict.

\begin{principle}[Relational resolution]
Indistinguishability under one restricted descriptive algebra does not imply identity under every richer descriptive algebra.
\end{principle}

The $\ell=5$ theorem supplies an exact example of this principle, but it does not provide an analogue of consciousness. Its quartic-flat directions are physical degeneracies of a truncated invariant energy, and its sextic witness resolves physical shape information. The broader use of the theorem is methodological: a finite descriptive truncation must not be treated as complete merely because no distinction is visible inside that truncation.
