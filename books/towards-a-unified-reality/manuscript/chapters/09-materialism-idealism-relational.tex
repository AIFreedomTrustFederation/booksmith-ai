\chapter{Materialism, Idealism, and Relational Ontology}

\section{The Direction of Predication}

Every ontology of consciousness arrives with an arrow, whether it acknowledges that arrow or hides it inside its vocabulary. The conventional grammar is

\[
P \longrightarrow E,
\]

where $P$ denotes the relevant physical-relational state and $E$ denotes experiential structure. Physical organization is treated as prior; experience appears downstream.

But this direction is not contained in the bare fact that physical and experiential states covary. At the level of observational factorization,

\[
p(P,E)
=
p(P)\,p(E\mid P)
=
p(E)\,p(P\mid E).
\]

The same joint distribution can therefore be written in either direction. Probability alone does not tell us which factorization represents causal predication.

This opens three conceptually distinct model families:

\[
\mathcal M_P:
\qquad
P\longrightarrow E,
\]

\[
p(P,E)
=
p(P)\,p(E\mid P),
\]

representing physical-first predication;

\[
\mathcal M_E:
\qquad
E\longrightarrow P,
\]

\[
p(P,E)
=
p(E)\,p(P\mid E),
\]

representing experiential-first predication; and

\[
\mathcal M_R:
\qquad
P\longleftarrow R\longrightarrow E,
\]

\[
p(P,E,R)
=
p(R)\,p(P\mid R)\,p(E\mid R),
\]

representing a common relational source from which physical exteriority and experiential interiority are separately projected.

These factorizations must not be mistaken for proofs of the corresponding ontologies. The first two probability factorizations are mathematically available for any ordinary joint distribution. Their ontological significance appears only when causal, temporal, interventional, and independence structure is added.

The question is therefore not merely whether $P$ predicts $E$, because prediction can run in either statistical direction. The question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Which directed structure survives intervention?}
}
\]

That is the point at which predication becomes an empirical problem.

\subsection{What Intervention Can Actually Establish}

Suppose an intervention alters a physical variable $P_i$ while appropriately controlling competing pathways, and an experiential indicator changes:

\[
p(E\mid \operatorname{do}(P_i=p_1))
\neq
p(E\mid \operatorname{do}(P_i=p_2)).
\]

This is genuine evidence that the manipulated physical variable lies on a causal route relevant to experience.

Anesthesia, pharmacological perturbation, electrical or magnetic stimulation, lesions, and related manipulations therefore matter profoundly. They establish that changing physical organization can change conditions associated with conscious experience.

But the inference must stop exactly where the intervention stops.

The result

\[
\operatorname{do}(P_i)\Rightarrow \Delta E
\]

does not by itself establish the complete ontological proposition

\[
P_{\mathrm{complete}}\longrightarrow E.
\]

It establishes a causal dependence involving the manipulated physical variable.

Likewise, such an intervention does constrain a simple one-way model

\[
E\longrightarrow P
\]

in which $P$ is purely downstream and has no return path to $E$. A consciousness-first ontology therefore cannot simply ignore physical intervention results. It must explain them.

It could posit reciprocal causation,

\[
E\rightleftarrows P,
\]

or embed both variables in a deeper relational dynamics,

\[
P\longleftarrow R\longrightarrow E,
\]

possibly with additional feedback,

\[
P\rightleftarrows R\rightleftarrows E.
\]

Or it could propose that what neuroscience labels $P$ is itself already one projection of a deeper structure rather than an ontologically independent cause.

Those are different theories and must make different predictions.

The important point is therefore more precise than saying that physical intervention cannot favor one ontology over another:

\[
\boxed{
\text{physical intervention constrains every viable ontology,}
\quad
\text{but does not automatically establish physical ontological priority.}
}
\]

A demonstrated arrow through a physical variable is not yet a theorem that all arrows originate in the physical.

\subsection{The Asymmetry of Experimental Access}

There is nevertheless a serious experimental asymmetry.

Physical variables can be manipulated through physical procedures. We can alter receptor binding, membrane potentials, network dynamics, sensory input, metabolism, cortical excitability, and many other measurable variables.

An alleged experiential variable cannot presently be isolated and manipulated independently of every physical realization in anything like the same straightforward manner.

That asymmetry limits causal identification.

It does not license the inference

\[
\text{only physically accessible interventions exist}
\quad\therefore\quad
\text{only physical causation exists}.
\]

Experimental accessibility and ontological priority are different propositions.

The first concerns what our intervention technology can manipulate.

The second concerns what reality fundamentally contains.

Confusing them would violate the same descriptive discipline developed earlier in this book: a limitation of the available measurement or intervention language must not be promoted into a limitation of reality itself.

\subsection{Conditional Independence as a Second Discriminator}

The competing models also differ in their proposed screening relations.

For a simple relational common-cause model,

\[
P\longleftarrow R\longrightarrow E,
\]

one expects, under the model's assumptions,

\[
P\perp E\mid R.
\]

Once the relevant relational source is completely specified, the residual dependence between its two projections should disappear.

A strong physical-sufficiency model instead seeks some sufficiently complete physical state $P^\ast$ such that

\[
E\perp R_{\mathrm{extra}}\mid P^\ast
\]

for any proposed additional variable $R_{\mathrm{extra}}$, while the physical description is also dynamically autonomous:

\[
\dot P^\ast=f(P^\ast).
\]

If every experiential distinction can be reconstructed from $P^\ast$, every additional candidate variable becomes redundant, and the physical dynamics close upon themselves, then the physical-first account has earned substantially more than a mere correlation.

Conversely, if two deeper states share the same proposed complete physical description,

\[
P^\ast(r_1)=P^\ast(r_2),
\]

while producing different empirically warranted experiential consequences,

\[
E(r_1)\neq E(r_2),
\]

then the proposed $P^\ast$ was not sufficient.

That would not automatically prove experiential-first ontology.

It would prove something more modest and more important:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the tested physical description was incomplete for the distinction at issue.}
}
\]

This is exactly the logic already encountered in the invariant audit.

\subsection{Why Wave Conjugation Belongs Here}

The mathematical image motivating the reversal of predication comes from complex conjugation.

Let

\[
\Psi=Ae^{i\theta}.
\]

Its conjugate is

\[
K\Psi
=
\Psi^\ast
=
Ae^{-i\theta}.
\]

Under the conditions developed earlier in this work,

\[
|\Psi^\ast|^2
=
|\Psi|^2,
\]

\[
C_G(\Psi^\ast)
=
C_G(\Psi),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Stab}(\Psi^\ast)
=
\operatorname{Stab}(\Psi).
\]

The coarse invariant form is therefore preserved.

Yet the directed current obeys

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf j_{\Psi^\ast}
=
-\mathbf j_\Psi.
}
\]

The same invariant appearance admits opposite directed flow.

This is not a derivation of idealism.

It does not imply

\[
P\rightarrow E
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E\rightarrow P.
\]

Nor does complex conjugation transform matter into consciousness.

Its significance is methodological.

It provides an exact mathematical demonstration that \emph{invariant form does not necessarily determine directional orientation}.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{invariant observational equivalence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{identity of directed relational structure}.
}
\]

That proposition belongs beside the descriptive-refinement theorem obtained from the quartic/sextic audit:

\[
\boxed{
\text{lower-resolution indistinguishability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{higher-resolution identity}.
}
\]

The two results identify different failures of description.

The quartic/sextic result exposes a failure of \emph{resolution}.

Wave conjugation exposes a potential failure of \emph{orientation}.

A descriptive language can therefore erase distinctions in at least two fundamentally different ways:

\[
\boxed{
\text{it can fail to resolve what differs,}
\quad\text{or fail to orient what runs oppositely.}
}
\]

\subsection{The Arrow Hidden Inside Ordinary Language}

This becomes especially important when mathematics is translated into ordinary philosophical language.

Consider the sentences:

\begin{quote}
Consciousness emerges from physical reality.
\end{quote}

and

\begin{quote}
Physical reality appears within consciousness.
\end{quote}

The nouns attract most of the philosophical dispute: \emph{consciousness}, \emph{matter}, \emph{physical}, \emph{experience}.

Yet the deepest disagreement may be carried by something linguistically much smaller:

\[
\boxed{\longrightarrow}
\]

The direction of predication differs.

One description says

\[
P\rightarrow E.
\]

The other says

\[
E\rightarrow P.
\]

A third refuses both privileged directions:

\[
P\leftarrow R\rightarrow E.
\]

The language audit must therefore examine verbs, dependency, and direction just as carefully as nouns.

Words such as \emph{produces}, \emph{emerges from}, \emph{generates}, \emph{manifests}, \emph{appears within}, \emph{projects}, and \emph{constitutes} are not stylistic ornaments. They encode causal or ontological arrows.

An ontology can therefore smuggle its conclusion into ordinary grammar before evidence has ever been examined.

The sentence

\begin{quote}
the brain produces consciousness
\end{quote}

already contains more than the empirical statement

\begin{quote}
changing some brain states changes some reliable indicators associated with consciousness.
\end{quote}

The first asserts an ontological direction.

The second reports a causal result.

The distance between those two sentences is precisely the territory this audit is designed to protect.

\subsection{Predication as a Model-Selection Problem}

The three broad possibilities should therefore remain distinct:

\[
\mathcal M_P:
\qquad
P\rightarrow E,
\]

\[
\mathcal M_E:
\qquad
E\rightarrow P,
\]

\[
\mathcal M_R:
\qquad
P\leftarrow R\rightarrow E.
\]

More elaborate models may include feedback, temporal recursion, latent variables, and different scales of description. The three structures above are therefore canonical starting families, not an exhaustive catalog of all possible ontologies.

Their competition cannot be settled by prose.

It requires evidence capable of distinguishing directed models: intervention, temporal precedence where meaningful, conditional independence, invariance across contexts, perturbation, predictive transfer, dynamical closure, and complexity-controlled comparison.

The physical-first model must earn the claim that an appropriately complete physical-relational state is sufficient and autonomous.

The experiential-first model must earn the claim that experiential structure has genuine predictive or causal priority that cannot be reproduced by physical or common-cause alternatives.

The relational model must earn the existence and necessity of a common structure $R$, rather than using $R$ merely as a label for whatever remains unexplained.

Every model therefore has something substantial to lose.

\subsection{The Direction-of-Predication Principle}

The audit leads to a general methodological principle:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Direction of Predication Principle}
}
\]

If multiple directed models reproduce the same observational structure, the direction of ontological predication is not licensed by observational equivalence alone. It must be earned through additional causal, interventional, dynamical, or independence structure.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
p(P,E)\not\Rightarrow P\rightarrow E,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
p(P,E)\not\Rightarrow E\rightarrow P,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
p(P,E)\not\Rightarrow P\leftarrow R\rightarrow E.
}
\]

The distribution gives the relation. It does not, by itself, give the direction or ontology of that relation.

This principle returns us directly to the larger thesis of the book. Platonic symmetry asks what remains invariant. The quartic/sextic audit asks at what descriptive resolution a difference can be seen. Wave dynamics asks how the state transforms. Conjugation asks whether directional structure can reverse while invariant form remains. Plain language asks which distinctions and directions its words have preserved, collapsed, or silently assumed. Ontology finally asks which of those descriptions has earned the right to say not merely how reality can be represented, but how reality actually predicates itself.

The answer cannot be read off from the arrow we inherited. The arrow itself is part of what must be tested.

\section{What $\mathcal M_P$ Must Earn: Physical-First Sufficiency}

The physical-first model is not entitled to ontological priority merely because physical variables are experimentally accessible. To become more than a convenient predictive ordering, $\mathcal M_P$ must earn a strong sufficiency claim. Let $P_{\mathrm{phys},\le t}^{\ast}$ denote a candidate physical-relational history rich enough to count as adequate for the phenomenon under study. Strong physical supervenience requires

\[
P_{\mathrm{phys},\le t}^{\ast(1)}
=
P_{\mathrm{phys},\le t}^{\ast(2)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_t^{(1)}=E_t^{(2)}.
\]

No two systems may be identical in the complete relevant physical-relational sense while differing experientially. But supervenience is only the beginning of what $\mathcal M_P$ must earn. A scientific physical-first model should also approach screening-off: once $P_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\ast}$ is supplied, adding an independently anchored experiential variable should not improve prediction of the relevant future physical observables,

\[
p(Y_{t+\Delta}\mid P_{\mathrm{phys},\le t}^{\ast},E_t,\operatorname{do}(A_t))
=
p(Y_{t+\Delta}\mid P_{\mathrm{phys},\le t}^{\ast},\operatorname{do}(A_t)),
\]

within declared tolerance and horizon. It should also approach dynamical closure,

\[
\dot P_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\ast}=f(P_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\ast}),
\]

or an explicitly specified stochastic or memory-bearing analogue when exact Markovian closure is inappropriate.

The burden is therefore not simply that physical interventions change phenomenological evidence. That fact already constrains every viable ontology. What $\mathcal M_P$ must additionally show is that systematic enrichment of the physical-relational state absorbs the distinctions for which an additional experiential or relational variable had appeared necessary. If residual structure disappears under principled enrichment, if the physical representation closes within tolerance, and if capacity-matched nonphysical or relational additions fail to improve transfer or intervention prediction, then the physical-first model has earned substantive support.

This formulation deliberately avoids caricaturing physicalism as a doctrine of tiny solid objects. A sophisticated $\mathcal M_P$ may be formulated in terms of fields, waves, relational states, nonlocal representations, higher-order invariants, dynamical symmetry breaking, or other mathematically rich structures. Relational mathematics does not defeat physicalism by changing the nouns. The issue is whether whatever counts as the complete physical-relational description is sufficient.

Accordingly, $\mathcal M_P$ loses explanatory force in the tested domain when increasingly adequate physical descriptions continue to leave independently anchored, transferable, interventionally relevant structure unexplained; when closure failure survives serious state reconstruction and hidden-variable controls; or when a complexity-matched alternative predicts and transfers better because it contains a genuinely necessary relation absent from $P_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\ast}$. Even then, the result is failure of the tested physical sufficiency claim, not automatic proof of idealism.

\section{What $\mathcal M_E$ Must Earn: Experiential-First Predication}

The experiential-first model faces an equally serious burden. It cannot win merely because experience is epistemically immediate, because every scientific observation is ultimately encountered in experience, or because the physical world is described through conscious concepts. Those facts concern access and description. They do not establish the causal or ontological arrow

\[
E\longrightarrow P.
\]

A viable $\mathcal M_E$ must identify an experiential structure with enough independent content to do explanatory work rather than simply renaming whatever the physical model has not yet explained. Schematically, an experiential-first dynamics might be represented as

\[
\dot\Xi=G_E(\Xi),
\]

with the public physical description generated or constrained through a projection

\[
P_{\mathrm{phys},t}=\Pi_E(\Xi_{\le t}).
\]

But the existence of such notation establishes only admissibility. The experiential structure must be independently anchored, its relation to physical observation must survive transfer, and its contribution must not collapse into a reparameterization of physical history.

Most importantly, $\mathcal M_E$ must explain the robust intervention evidence already discussed. A simple acyclic model in which $E$ is the sole upstream cause and $P$ is purely downstream is constrained by findings of the form

\[
\operatorname{do}(P_i)\Rightarrow\Delta Z_\varphi,
\]

where $Z_\varphi$ denotes independently constructed phenomenological evidence. A serious experiential-first theory must therefore specify why manipulating a physical variable changes the experiential evidence. It may require reciprocal coupling,

\[
E\rightleftarrows P,
\]

or a deeper dynamics in which the manipulated physical variable is itself part of the route through which experiential organization is stabilized or expressed. Once feedback is introduced, however, the theory must earn those additional arrows through predictions that distinguish it from $\mathcal M_P$ and $\mathcal M_R$ rather than using feedback as an unfalsifiable escape hatch.

The Platonic and wave results do not supply this evidence. Platonic invariance shows how form can persist through changing realization; wave conjugation shows how directed structure can reverse while selected invariants remain fixed. Neither theorem establishes that experience predicates physical reality. They only prevent the opposite direction from being granted by descriptive habit alone.

For $\mathcal M_E$ to gain scientific priority, an experiential variable or law would need to contribute reproducible predictive, interventional, or independence structure that physical-first and common-source alternatives cannot reconstruct under matched capacity. If experiential priority never generates a discriminating consequence, then it remains a coherent metaphysical interpretation rather than an empirically earned direction of predication.

\section{What $\mathcal M_R$ Must Earn: A Common Relational Source}

The relational model begins from neither physical nor experiential priority. Let $r\in\mathscr R$ and define

\[
P_{\mathrm{phys}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r),
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{exp}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{int}}(r),
\]

so that

\[
\mathcal M_R:\qquad
P_{\mathrm{phys}}\longleftarrow\mathscr R\longrightarrow E_{\mathrm{exp}}.
\]

This notation has intuitive force because it allows exterior and interior descriptions to differ without positing two independent substances. But the diagram is almost vacuous unless $\mathscr R$ earns independent structure. A latent space can always be made large enough to store two collections of variables. The existence of a common container therefore proves nothing.

A scientifically meaningful $\mathcal M_R$ must satisfy at least three burdens. First, the common state must be constrained independently of the two outcomes it is supposed to explain. Second, its projections must encode nontrivial relations that cannot be reproduced by simply concatenating physical and phenomenological variables. Third, the relational structure must generate testable screening, transfer, intervention, or closure consequences that outperform capacity-matched alternatives.

A local rank condition provides a minimal mathematical test of complementarity. If $n=\dim\mathscr R$ and

\[
\operatorname{rank}D\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}<n,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}D\Pi_{\mathrm{int}}<n,
\]

neither projection locally reconstructs the underlying state. If the joint map satisfies

\[
\operatorname{rank}D(\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}},\Pi_{\mathrm{int}})=n,
\]

then the pair is locally sufficient even though each projection is individually incomplete.

This establishes complementary coordinates, not phenomenal interiority. The interpretation of $\Pi_{\mathrm{int}}$ as experiential must be earned independently through the occupancy program developed in the next chapter. Likewise, the common-cause screening relation

\[
P\perp E\mid R
\]

is a prediction only after $R$ has been defined independently enough to make the conditional-independence claim nontrivial. A relational model that manufactures $R$ after seeing both $P$ and $E$ has not discovered a common source; it has encoded the joint data.

The defeat condition is therefore strict. If a capacity-matched physical latent model reconstructs the same joint structure, if $R$ fails cross-context transfer, if the proposed screening relation does not hold, or if $R$ contributes no interventionally relevant prediction beyond its component projections, then $\mathcal M_R$ has not earned explanatory priority.

\section{Dynamical Closure as a Discriminator}

Dynamical closure is not evidence reserved for one ontology; it is a discriminator among them. Suppose the underlying state evolves by

\[
\dot r=F(r).
\]

The exterior projection obeys

\[
\dot P_{\mathrm{phys}}=D\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r)F(r).
\]

For the projected physical state to possess a closed autonomous dynamics, there must exist $f$ such that

\[
\dot P_{\mathrm{phys}}=f(P_{\mathrm{phys}}).
\]

If two underlying states satisfy

\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r_1)=\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r_2)
\]

but

\[
D\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r_1)F(r_1)
\neq
D\Pi_{\mathrm{ext}}(r_2)F(r_2),
\]

then no exact autonomous law exists on that chosen projection.

That result alone does not favor $\mathcal M_E$ or $\mathcal M_R$, because an incomplete physical coarse-graining produces the same mathematical symptom. A hidden physical phase, a missing slow variable, memory, inadequate state reconstruction, or measurement noise can all make an otherwise physical system appear nonclosed. The correct test is therefore progressive enrichment and approximate closure under declared tolerance, not a single failure of a reduced model.

The same discipline applies to the other models. If an experiential-first state is claimed to be generatively complete, its dynamics must explain why additional physical information appears causally necessary under intervention. If a relational state is claimed to be the common source, the joint dynamics on $(P,E)$ or on the independently specified $R$ must outperform simpler closures rather than merely accommodate them after the fact.

Closure therefore becomes one part of the shared burden structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a model earns priority only when its chosen state is sufficient for the distinctions and transitions it claims to generate.}
}
\]

\section{Difference Without Absolute Separation}

The relational framework also clarifies a philosophical confusion that often enters discussions of duality. Mathematical difference does not imply absolute ontological separation. In symbols,

\[
A\neq B
\]

does not entail that $A$ and $B$ belong to unrelated substances or disconnected domains. Two projections may differ because they preserve different relational information. A state and its conjugate may differ while sharing density and symmetry. Two coordinates may differ while belonging to one underlying manifold. None of these examples proves dual-aspect monism, but all show that difference alone does not force substance dualism.

The same warning applies in the other direction. Shared origin does not prove shared ontology; statistical dependence does not prove causal direction; complementarity does not prove consciousness; and a successful physical intervention does not by itself prove that the physical is ontologically first.

\section{The Burdens Compared}

The three model families can now be stated not as philosophical positions followed by an unrelated empirical program, but as competing burden structures.

\[
\mathcal M_P:\quad P\rightarrow E
\]

must earn physical-relational sufficiency, screening-off, approximate closure, intervention coverage, and the disappearance of any independently anchored residual under principled enrichment.

\[
\mathcal M_E:\quad E\rightarrow P
\]

must earn experiential structure independently of physical description, explain the causal force of physical interventions, and provide discriminating predictions that cannot be reconstructed by physical-first or common-source models.

\[
\mathcal M_R:\quad P\leftarrow R\rightarrow E
\]

must earn an independently constrained common state, nontrivial complementary projections, appropriate conditional-independence or closure structure, and predictive or interventional advantage beyond capacity-matched alternatives.

More elaborate reciprocal models may ultimately fit reality better than any of these canonical starting graphs. If so, the graph should become more complicated because the evidence requires additional arrows, not because a preferred ontology needs protection from defeat.

The ontological contest therefore remains genuinely open, but it is no longer merely verbal. Each model now inherits the same discipline introduced in the Direction-of-Predication audit: its arrow, its state variables, and its claims of sufficiency must be earned by evidence rather than smuggled into the grammar that states the theory. The next chapter tightens that burden further by asking what any ontology must establish before one of its mathematical structures deserves the description of consciousness.
