\chapter*{How to Read This Book}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to Read This Book}

The book is written as one argument rather than as a collection of disconnected mathematical curiosities. Chapter 1 establishes the language audit and introduces two distinct ways a description can fail: it can collapse differences it lacks the resolution to represent, or it can preserve coarse appearance while concealing or assuming directional orientation. Chapter 2 develops relational persistence. Chapters 3 through 7 then supply the mathematical audit through oscillator dynamics, Platonic symmetry, the exact $\ell=5$ quartic theorem, sixth-order invariant resolution, geometry, wave dynamics, and complex conjugation. Chapter 8 is the methodological hinge: it separates descriptive adequacy, directional adequacy, and ontological sufficiency. Chapter 9 asks which direction of predication is actually earned and formulates physical-first, experiential-first, and relational model families. Chapter 10 asks what an ontology must earn before any of its structures deserve the description of consciousness. Chapter 11 retains one primary empirical protocol and uses it to compare predication, sufficiency, closure, transfer, and model competition. Chapter 12 returns to the original question with the mathematical, linguistic, causal, and ontological audits in hand.

Readers interested primarily in the strongest exact mathematics can move directly from Chapters 4 through 7 to Appendix B. The $\ell=5$ sextic result is now closed at the level of algebraic resolving capability: the exact invariant count gives four primitive degree-six directions beyond $I_2\operatorname{Inv}_4$, and an independently reconstructed explicit invariant $J_{4,8}$ has positive-definite intrinsic Hessian on both physical quartic-flat directions. Appendix B records the exact distinction between the ambient straight-line Hessian and the constrained Hessian, including the second-order correction required to remain on $S^{10}\cap\{B_2=0\}$. The PDE-specific fifth-order amplitude reduction remains open and is not supplied by the invariant theorem.

Chapter 7 should not be read merely as a wave-theory appendix to the invariant material. The quartic/sextic result and the conjugation result expose two different limitations of description. The first is a failure of resolution: a lower-order invariant language can identify states a richer algebra separates. The second is a failure of orientation: a chosen set of invariant observables can remain unchanged while a directed quantity reverses. Their relation is methodological rather than metaphysical. One tells us that a language may fail to resolve what differs; the other tells us that a language may fail to orient what runs oppositely.

Readers interested primarily in the physical--experiential inference problem should still read Chapters 7 and 8 before Chapters 9 through 11. The causal question does not begin with the arrow $P\to E$ already granted. Chapter 9 treats

\[
\mathcal M_P:\ P\longrightarrow E,
\qquad
\mathcal M_E:\ E\longrightarrow P,
\qquad
\mathcal M_R:\ P\longleftarrow R\longrightarrow E
\]

as competing starting families whose burdens must be earned rather than philosophical positions stated first and tested later. More elaborate reciprocal and feedback models may be required by data, but the three canonical structures make the direction of predication explicit enough to audit.

One governing rule sits above the book's object-level boundaries:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Direction cannot be inferred merely from relation.}
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p(X,Y)\nRightarrow X\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
p(X,Y)\nRightarrow Y\longrightarrow X.
\]

This is not a fifth peer beside the other principles. It is the cross-cutting rule that explains why the others must remain separate. Relation, covariance, formal correspondence, and observational equivalence can all be genuine while causal or ontological direction remains underdetermined.

Beneath that governing rule, four boundaries recur throughout the book. First, indistinguishability under a restricted descriptive algebra does not imply identity under every richer descriptive algebra. Second, invariant observational equivalence does not imply identity of directed relational structure. Third, descriptive-language adequacy does not imply ontological sufficiency. Fourth, mathematical admissibility does not imply phenomenal occupancy. The first two belong to the mathematical and language audit; the third governs the movement from description to ontology; the fourth governs the movement from formal consciousness models to claims about actual experience.

Appendix A freezes notation and the status of claims. Appendix B preserves the exact $\ell=5$ technical ledger and the ambient-versus-intrinsic Hessian correction. Appendix C isolates the conditional antiunitary hypothesis so that wave-orientation reversal cannot silently become an ontological-arrow reversal. Appendix D states defeaters and limitations in the strongest form the framework currently supports. Appendix E positions the program relative to symmetry and bifurcation theory, structural and process traditions, causal and physicalist consciousness theories, integrated information theory, no-report paradigms, perturbational methods, and related mathematical and philosophical work. Appendix F maps the monograph into the wider Federation canon. Appendix G is deliberately different: it is an expository track for recursive relational geometry, where established mathematics, framework definitions, and interpretive propositions are typographically and epistemically separated so that a useful model of multiscale organization does not acquire theorem-level or ontological authority merely from formal notation.

The reader should therefore keep one sequence in view from beginning to end:

\[
\text{form}
\longrightarrow
\text{resolution}
\longrightarrow
\text{orientation}
\longrightarrow
\text{predication}
\longrightarrow
\text{ontology}.
\]

The arrows in that sequence indicate the order of the audit, not a metaphysical chain of production. The entire purpose of the book is to prevent an explanatory arrow from being mistaken for a discovered one before the evidence has earned its direction.
